ODIN privacy policy

How the ODIN app and server handle your information.

Draft for review: 2 items marked UNSURE need a decision before this page goes live.

Effective date: (set on publication)

The short version

  • Outland Dynamics runs no servers for ODIN and collects nothing about you. We have no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no crash-reporting service. The only thing we ever receive is an email you choose to send us (see "Reporting a problem").
  • The ODIN app talks to one place: the ODIN server you (or the person who invited you) run, for example on a home NAS. Everything ODIN knows about you is stored on that server or on your phone, not with us.
  • That server may look up public information about your titles (show and movie names, book titles and authors) from a few public catalogues, so it can show posters, summaries and cast. It never sends your name, email, files, folder paths or viewing history. The server owner can turn this off.

Who we are

ODIN is made by Outland Dynamics. You buy ODIN once through Apple's App Store or Google Play (on iPhone, after an optional 7-day free trial). The payment is handled by Apple or Google under their own privacy policies. We do not receive your card details, name or email.

The app checks your purchase (and on iPhone, your trial) on your phone, by asking Apple or Google directly. It never sends that check to us, and your ODIN server never checks with us. Apple and Google give us sales reports (for example how many purchases there are, and in which countries), which do not identify you.

Contact: support@outlanddynamics.com

What the app does with your data

The app connects only to the server address you type in, scan, or open from an invitation link. It keeps on your phone:

  • your sign-in for that server (a session key) and the server's security fingerprint, in the phone's secure storage (iOS Keychain or Android Keystore);
  • in the app's ordinary storage: the server address, your app settings, some personal lists kept only on the phone (for example book notes and bookmarks, your watchlist, recent searches, reading goal and layout choices), and a copy of the last lists the server sent (titles and file names), so screens open quickly.

The app does not save films, music, books or comics on the phone.

Phone backups. Nothing here goes to us; a backup is your own, with Apple or Google or on your computer.

  • iPhone. The app's ordinary storage is left out of iCloud and computer backups (the storage component the app uses excludes its folder from backups, and we have not changed that). Your sign-in is saved in the Keychain as "this device only": Apple does not restore such items onto a different phone, so on a new iPhone you sign in again and your phone-only lists start empty. UNSURE whether a direct iPhone-to-iPhone transfer (Quick Start) copies the ordinary storage; not tested.
  • iPhone, after deleting the app. Deleting the app removes its ordinary storage. iOS can keep Keychain items of a deleted app, so the saved sign-in and the server's fingerprint may stay in the phone's Keychain, where no other app can read them. If you install ODIN again, the first time it opens it deletes that leftover sign-in before using anything, so you sign in again. An update of the app keeps your sign-in. Deleting the leftover on the phone does not sign the device out on the server: to end that sign-in too, sign out in the app before deleting it, or remove the device from the server's device list.
  • Android. The app's data is left out of Google's cloud backup and out of phone-to-phone transfer when you set up a new phone (allowBackup: false, plus data-extraction rules that exclude everything from both). On a new phone you sign in again, and your settings and phone-only lists start empty. Deleting the app removes its storage and the key that protects the saved sign-in.

The app has no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting code, and no third-party tracking SDKs.

The phone does not contact any other site to show you pictures. TV show artwork (from TVmaze), movie posters and book covers are all fetched by your server and passed to the phone, so those sites never see your phone's internet address. Your server keeps copies in its own data folder (see below).

If you tap a link in the app (for example "TVmaze", a book's source page, the install guide or this policy), it opens in your browser and that website's own privacy policy applies.

What your ODIN server stores

The server belongs to its owner, not to us. It keeps its data in its own database and data folder on the machine it runs on (typically two Docker volumes on a NAS), plus any backups the owner makes. We cannot see or reach it.

It stores:

  • Accounts: the owner, and each invited person's first name, email address and password (as a one-way scrypt hash, never the password itself).
  • Invitations: the invited email address, which libraries were shared, and when the invitation was sent, accepted or cancelled. The invitation code is stored only as a hash.
  • Signed-in devices: a device name, when it signed in and was last used (the session key is stored only as a hash).
  • Your activity, per person: watch and listening progress, ratings, reading status (want to read, reading, read), TV playlists and comic reading orders.
  • Library information: the owner's library names, folder locations, and the catalogue the server builds from the files (titles, years, episode lists, covers, posters and TV artwork it downloaded, and so on). Original media files are never changed.
  • Artwork from your own files: for movies and shows without online artwork, the server reads poster images beside the media (such as poster.jpg or folder.jpg) and cover art stored inside video files, and keeps resized copies in its data folder. This is read on the server only; nothing about these files is sent anywhere.
  • Audiobook details from your own files: the server reads the tags inside audiobook files (.m4b, .m4a, .mp3: title, author, narrator, series, summary, year) and keeps them with the book's information. When the book's first file holds a picture, that picture is its cover, before any online one, and a resized copy is kept in the server's data folder. Online covers are used only for books whose file has no picture. The files are only read, never changed. Of the tags, only the title and author are sent anywhere, as search words (see below).
  • Server logs: for each request, the time, the route (which kind of request it was), the result code and the device's network (IP) address, written to the server's console log (for example docker logs). Logs hold no names, email addresses, passwords, sign-in keys or titles. With the install guide's set-up, Docker keeps at most about 30 MB of log for each container (three 10 MB files) and drops the oldest lines as new ones arrive, so how far back the log goes depends on how busy the server is, not on a set number of days. The log is also deleted when the container is replaced, for example by an update. An owner who runs the server another way has whatever log settings they chose.

Invited people see only the libraries shared with them. Other people can see an average rating only once at least three people have rated an item, so one person's exact score is not revealed.

How to delete your data

  • Delete your account yourself. In the app: Home → Account → Delete my account, enter your password and confirm. This deletes, on that server, at once and for good: your sign-in (first name, email and password hash) and every device signed in with it, which libraries you can see, your watch and listening progress, ratings, reading status, TV playlists, the comic reading orders you made (people who follow them lose them too), any password reset codes, and the invitations sent to your email address. The media and other people's data are not touched. Nothing identifying you is kept.
  • Or ask the server owner, who can remove you in Sharing (Remove from server). That deletes exactly the same data.
  • On your phone: notes, bookmarks, your watchlist and other lists kept only on the phone are deleted when you delete the app.
  • The server owner cannot delete their own account in the app; to remove ODIN and all its data, they follow the install guide's Uninstall section.
  • Backups the owner already made still hold your data for a while. Deleting an account removes it from the server's live database at once. It does not reach into backup files. A backup taken before the deletion still contains your first name, email address, password hash and activity. The server keeps the newest 7 backups of each kind (nightly, before an update, made by hand, before a restore) and deletes older ones, so with nightly backups on, a nightly copy is gone after about a week; the other kinds stay until 7 newer ones of that kind exist, which can take much longer. Copies the owner moved elsewhere (another disk or computer) stay until the owner deletes them. If the owner restores a backup from before your deletion, your account comes back; ask the owner to delete it again. You can ask the owner to delete old backups.
  • Logs do not contain your name or email address, so there is nothing of that kind to remove. Lines with your device's network address stay until the log rolls over (see "Server logs" above).
  • UNSURE the database program (PostgreSQL) writes its own log, kept under the same 30 MB limit. It normally records start-up and errors only; whether an error line can ever include a value such as an email address has not been checked.

Who the server contacts

All of these are contacted by the server, not by Outland Dynamics, and only for information about titles. No personal data is sent: no names, emails, file names, folder paths, viewing history or device information. Requests are spaced out and use fixed addresses.

Audible and Audnexus: Audible's catalogue search is not an official, documented service for other apps. Until that switch is built they are on, as the table says.

Email. If the owner sets up email (UMP_SMTP_*), invitation emails are sent through the owner's own mail account (for example their Gmail). The invitation contains the server address and a one-time code. Without email set up, the owner sends the invitation by hand.

Video transcoding. If the owner wants transcoding, they download Cisco's OpenH264 library themselves and copy it to the server. The server does not download it or contact Cisco.

The server does not contact Outland Dynamics for licences, updates or anything else.

Reporting a problem

"Report a problem" (in About ODIN, and on the "Something went wrong" screen) opens your own email app with a message addressed to support@outlanddynamics.com that you can read and edit before sending. If the phone has no email app, ODIN shows the address and the same text with a Copy button instead. Nothing is sent unless you send that email yourself.

The message contains the app version and build, the server version, and your phone's system and its version. Sent from the "Something went wrong" screen, it also contains the error's name and message and the list of app screens involved. For the server owner it also contains a diagnostics summary: the server's address, disk space, the media folder locations, library names with item counts and last scan results, database size, backup times and transcoding status. Passwords, sign-in keys and email addresses are removed from it. When you send it, we also receive what any email carries: your email address and whatever you write.

Children

ODIN is not directed at children under 13, and Outland Dynamics does not knowingly collect data from anyone, including children. Whether a child uses ODIN, and what media they can see, is up to the server owner and the child's parent or guardian.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at the same address with a new date. The history of changes is kept in the project's repository.
