Installing ODIN

The install guide has the steps for joining a server you were invited to, and for setting up your own server, remote access, backups and updates.

Common questions

Do I need an account with Outland Dynamics?

No. You sign in to your own ODIN server, or to the server of the person who invited you. We don't run accounts or servers for ODIN.

Someone invited me. How do I join?

Open the invitation email on your phone and tap the link. It opens ODIN and fills in the server for you. Then enter your first name, the email the invitation was sent to, and a password.

I forgot my password.

On the sign-in screen, tap "Forgot password?" and enter your email. If the server owner hasn't set up email, ask them for a reset code from Sharing.

How do I delete my account?

In the app, go to Home → Account → Delete my account, enter your password and confirm. This removes your account and your activity from that server at once. You can also ask the server's owner to remove you.

A video won't play or keeps buffering.

Open the player's Quality menu and choose a lower option, such as 720p. If you're away from home, check that your connection to the server is on. If it still fails, use Report a problem so the server's details come with your message.

Which phones are supported?

iPhones on iOS 17 or later and Android phones on Android 7.0 or later. During the beta the app comes through TestFlight on iPhone and a Google Play test link on Android. The iPhone version is newer and has more rough edges.

Does ODIN work away from home?

Yes, once the server's owner sets it up. ODIN only answers the home network by default; away from home it uses Tailscale, a separate free app that privately connects your phone to the server. Nothing is opened on the owner's router. See Using ODIN away from home.

What files does ODIN play and read?

Video: common files such as MP4, M4V, MOV and MKV play as they are or are repackaged by the server. Older or unusual video (AVI, WMV, MPEG-2 and similar) has to be converted, which the owner turns on in the server. Comics: CBZ and CBR, but not CB7 or password-protected files. Ebooks: EPUB; PDFs appear on the shelf but can't be read in the app yet. Audiobooks: M4B, MP3 and M4A. Music: MP3, FLAC, M4A, OGG and Opus.

What do I need to run my own server?

A NAS or Linux computer that stays on and runs Docker, your media folders, and a phone with the ODIN app. Windows isn't supported as a server during the beta. The server isn't a public download yet; the install guide explains the steps and how to get it.

How do I update the server?

Get the new version, then start the server again from its folder. Libraries, accounts and progress stay, phones stay paired, and the server takes a safety backup first when the update changes its database. See Updating the server.

How do I back up ODIN?

The server can take a backup on demand or every night. A backup holds the database and the server's identity, not your media. Copy backups to another disk or computer and keep them private. See Backups.

Will ODIN change or move my files?

No. The server only reads your media. Artwork, conversions and indexes go in the server's own data folder.

What does ODIN cost?

ODIN is planned as a one-time $12.99 purchase per person through the App Store or Google Play. On iPhone it's a free download with a 7-day free trial first; on Android it's a paid app. No subscription, and updates are included. During the beta it's free for invited testers.
